## Quality Attributes and Metrics Definitions

1.  **Privacy**: In IoT systems, privacy means user control over their pervasive data (location, biometrics, behavioral patterns, etc.) collected by connected devices. For privacy QA, we adopt LINDDUN framework to define the important metrics for quantifying privacy. These metrics are linking, identifying, non-repudiation, detecting, data disclosure, unawareness, and non-compliance.
2.  **Reliability**: This measures are used to assess the degree to which a system, product or component performs specified functions under specified conditions for a specified period of time. We selected four metrics under this QA which are faultlessness, availability, fault tolerance, and recoverability.
3.  **Robustness**: This is the ability of the system to operate correctly and deliver the intended service even in the presence of unexpected or abnormal conditions, such as invalid inputs, component failures, or stressful environmental disturbances. Sample metrics are adaptability and resilience.
4.  **Security**: This measures are used to assess the degree to which a system protects information and data. We selected four important metrics to measure how secure AIoT system is. These metrics are confidentiality, integrity, availability, and authenticity.
5.  **Reproducibility**: This is the ability to consistently obtain the same results or system behavior when using the same input data, software, and computational environment, even if the experiment is run by a different team. Three key objective metrics are selected which are data reproducibility, method reproducibility, and results reproducibility.
6.  **Accountability**: This refers to establishing mechanisms that ensure responsibility for the system’s actions, decisions, and outcomes throughout its life-cycle. We identified two objective metrics for this QA which are auditability and non-repudiation.
7.  **Fairness**: This is the ability of the system to ensure equitable, impartial, and non-discriminatory treatment of individuals and groups. Under this QA, we selected one objective and subjective metrics which are substantive fairness and perceived fairness respectively.
8.  **Transparency**: This refers to the extent to which the system’s processes, decisions, and data usage are understandable and accessible to stakeholders. It enables users to understand how the system operates and what factors influence its outputs. Two important objective metrics are identified under this QA, which are explainability and traceability. 
9.  **Human Agency**: Human agency and oversight requires that users should be able to understand and interact with AI systems in a reasonable manner. This also include ability of the system to respect user's fundamental rights. We identified three subjective metrics under this QA (i.e., learnability, operability, and human autonomy).
10. **Functional Suitability**: This is used to assess the degree to which a product or system provides functions that meet stated and implied needs when used under specified conditions. We identified three important objective metrics such as functional correctness, functional completeness, and functional appropriateness.
11. **Safety**: This is the capability of a system under defined conditions to avoid a state in which human life, health, property, or the environment is endangered. Two subjective metrics are defined including human and societal safety.
